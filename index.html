Local knowledge

The survival kit

Bits the tourist sites won't tell you, learned the hard way so you don't have to.

🚗

Two sets of wheels

Get a normal hire car from YLW for the Banff run and day trips — your UK licence is valid for visitors (grab an IDP to be safe), and drive on the right. Then book the exotic-car day separately (Jul 6) — those are reserved through the supercar operators, not your rental desk.

🏒

No hockey in July, sorry

Straight up: the NHL is off-season (it ends mid-June) and Kelowna's rinks close for summer — there's no live ice hockey to watch on your dates, anywhere nearby. Your live-sport fix is the BC Lions on Jul 4. For a hockey hit, catch a game replay over wings at a downtown sports bar.

🍙

The anime cluster

Better than you'd expect for a lake town: Izakaya (anime bar), Round1 (Japanese arcade), Nani (anime store) and Original 151 (Pokémon) sit together around Orchard Park / Fairfield Commons — knock them out in one afternoon loop.

💵

Money & tax maths

Prices on shelves are before tax (~12% in BC, 5% in AB) and tip 15–20% on top in bars/restaurants. Tap-to-pay is everywhere. Budget roughly CA$150–250/day pp excluding flights & the big-ticket activities.

🍺

Drinking age trap

BC is 19, Alberta is 18 — carry your passport, they card hard. Okanagan craft beer is unreal; pace yourself on the wine-tour bus or day two will hurt.

☀️

Hotter & drier than you think

The Okanagan is semi-desert — 30–35°C in July, intense sun, cold lake. SPF, hats, water. The Rockies are 10°C cooler and can turn fast: pack one warm layer + a rain shell. Wildfire smoke is possible — check air quality.

🏔️

Banff needs a pass

You need a Parks Canada day pass per vehicle to stop in the national parks (buy online). Book Banff's car-free attractions ahead and start hikes early — car parks fill by 9am.
